EDA Findings — Statistical Insights

⚧️
Gender Was the #1 Survival Predictor
Females survived at 74.2% vs males at just 18.9% — a 3.9× difference. The "Women and children first" protocol was enforced strictly. Sex had the highest correlation with survival after feature encoding.
🎫
Socioeconomic Class Drove Access to Lifeboats
1st class passengers survived at 62.9% vs 3rd class at 24.2%. Cabin location (upper decks) and crew prioritisation gave wealthy passengers a structural advantage in lifeboat access.
💷
Fare Price Proxies Survival Chance
Survivors paid an average fare of $48.40 vs non-survivors' $22.12. High-fare passengers (>£50) survived at 65.2% — 2.1× higher than low-fare passengers. Fare and Pclass show -0.55 correlation.
👨‍👩‍👧
Small Families Had the Best Odds
Family size of 3 achieved peak survival at 72.4%. Solo travellers at 30.4% and large families (5+) at 13.6% — the inverted-U pattern shows small groups could coordinate and evacuate efficiently.
👶
Children Were Prioritised in Evacuation
The 0–18 age group survived at 54.0% — the highest of any age bracket. Elderly passengers (60+) had the worst survival rate at 23.1%. Age had a -0.07 correlation with survival — weak but directionally consistent.
🧩
Feature Engineering Boosted ML Accuracy
Adding Family_Size, High_Fare, and Age_Group features improved model accuracy from 79.1% (raw features only) to 83.7% — a +4.6% gain. Domain-driven feature creation matters as much as algorithm choice.
💼

Business Recommendations — Translating Findings to Action

01
Redesign Emergency Priority Protocols
The data clearly shows evacuation priority was gender and class-based. Modern maritime law (SOLAS) should be updated to reflect mobility-based priority (children, elderly, people with disabilities) rather than gender or ticket class, ensuring fairer outcomes.
02
Lower-Deck Safety Infrastructure Investment
3rd class passengers at 24.2% survival were structurally disadvantaged. Modern ship design should mandate equal lifeboat access routes from all deck levels — particularly from lower-deck passenger cabins with longer evacuation paths.
03
Solo Traveller Emergency Buddy System
Solo passengers survived at just 30.4% vs small groups at 55–72%. Cruise lines could implement onboarding buddy systems or muster station group assignments to replicate the coordination advantage that families and small groups naturally possessed.
04
Predictive Risk Scoring for Passenger Safety
The Random Forest model achieves 83.7% accuracy using only passenger manifest data. A real-time risk scoring system — incorporating cabin location, age, group size, and boarding port — could pre-assign evacuation routes and improve muster drill effectiveness.
05
Age-Based Emergency Crew Allocation
Elderly passengers (60+) had the lowest survival at 23.1%. Senior-age cabin zones should have dedicated emergency crew pre-assigned at departure, with shorter evacuation distances to lifeboat stations built into ship layout planning.
06
Insurance Premium Modelling Application
Survival-correlated features (class, cabin zone, family size, age) could inform maritime insurance risk models. Insurers could use demographic and booking data at time of policy to more accurately price passenger safety exposure on cruise and ferry routes.
